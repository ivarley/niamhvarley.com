Lesson 1

Draw from your shoulder

Skill
Confident lines
You'll need
Nothing but the app
Time
15 minutes

Warm-up

Zoom out until you have a big empty area. Pick the thinnest pen.

2:00

The exercise

  1. Put two dots on the canvas, far apart. Hover your pencil above the surface and swing it between them three or four times without touching down. Then commit: one fast stroke, straight through. Don't creep towards the second dot — aim at it and go.
  2. Do that thirty times, in every direction.
  3. Now ovals. Draw each one as a single loop that goes round three times without lifting the pencil. Twenty of them, all sizes.

What to look for

Fast lines come out straighter than slow ones. Slow careful lines wobble. This is annoying and it's true, and it stays true forever.

Done means

Thirty lines and twenty ovals are on the canvas. Straightness is not the point yet.

In Endless Paper

Keep the canvas zoomed out. The bigger the movement, the more your arm does the work instead of your fingers.

For the grown-up

This is a physical habit, not an art skill — drawing from the shoulder and elbow rather than the wrist. It's the single quickest thing that makes a drawing look assured rather than tentative. Worth revisiting as a warm-up before any later lesson. Do it yourself; adults are usually worse at it than children.
