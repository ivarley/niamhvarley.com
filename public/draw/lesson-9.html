Lesson 9

Strokes that wrap

Skill
Hatching that describes form
You'll need
The same lamp, a tin can and a box
Time
15 minutes

Warm-up

Draw five squares in a row. Hatch them from nearly white to nearly solid black, so they step evenly. That's a value scale, and it's the reference you'll use from now on.

3:00

The exercise

  1. Light the tin can. Hatch it — but every stroke has to curve around the can, the way a stripe would if you painted one on it. Straight vertical strokes will make it look flat.
  2. Light the box. Hatch each face separately, straight strokes, and give each face a different density. Never let strokes run across a corner from one face to the next.
  3. For the darkest areas on both, go over your hatching a second time at a different angle — cross-hatching.

What to look for

The direction of your strokes tells the viewer the shape of the surface, before they've even registered how dark it is.

Done means

A value scale, a hatched can, a hatched box.

In Endless Paper

Zoom in to lay strokes down accurately, then zoom out to check the overall value. Hatching always looks wrong up close.

For the grown-up

Corners are the giveaway. If strokes run across an edge, the box goes soft. Easy thing to point at without commenting on the drawing's quality.
