Lesson 8

Light on a ball

Skill
Reading light
You'll need
A desk lamp, an egg or an orange or a plain ball, a dim room
Time
20 minutes

Warm-up

Draw a ball. Yes, just an oval.

2:00

The exercise

  1. Put the ball on the table. Turn off the main light. Point the lamp at it from one side, fairly low down.

  2. Before drawing anything, find these five things with your finger:

    • the highlight — the tiny bright spot where the lamp is reflecting
    • the midtone — the general lit side
    • the core shadow — a dark band on the dark side, and here's the surprise: it is not at the outer edge, it sits just inside it
    • the reflected light — the rim beyond the core shadow, slightly lighter again, because light is bouncing off the table back onto the ball
    • the cast shadow — the ball's shadow on the table, darkest right where the ball touches and softer as it stretches away
  3. Now draw it, with hatching: parallel pen strokes, packed tight where it's dark, spread out where it's light, none at all on the highlight.

What to look for

The rim of the ball is not the darkest part. Get that one thing right and a circle turns into a sphere.

Done means

One ball drawn with all five things attempted.

In Endless Paper

No smudging and no soft brush, so all shading is hatching. That's a real technique, not a workaround — it's how ink drawings have been shaded for five hundred years.

For the grown-up

This is usually the lesson where it clicks and drawing suddenly feels like magic. Set the lamp up before you call her over; fiddling with lighting eats the whole session. One lamp only — two light sources destroy the effect.
