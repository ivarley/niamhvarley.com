Lesson 7

Draw the same dog twice

Skill
Catching yourself drawing from memory
You'll need
A photo of a dog, or a real one
Time
20 minutes

Warm-up

None. Go straight in.

The exercise

  1. Without looking at anything, draw a dog in sixty seconds. Whatever comes out.
  2. Now put a photo of a real dog next to you and build a new one out of lesson 6's solids: a ball for the ribcage, a smaller ball for the hips, tubes for the legs and neck, a box for the muzzle. Get the solids in the right places and the right sizes before you draw a single hair or eye.
  3. Then, and only then, draw the outline over the top of the solids.
  4. Keep both drawings side by side. Don't delete the first one.

What to look for

The first dog is a symbol — the dog-shape you've had stored in your head since you were about five, and you've been drawing it ever since without noticing. The second one is an actual animal. Every subject you've ever drawn a lot has a symbol like this hiding behind it. Horses. Eyes. Trees. Hands.

Done means

Two dogs, both kept.

In Endless Paper

Draw the solids in a light colour and the outline over the top in black, so you can see the scaffolding underneath.

For the grown-up

This is the pivotal lesson of the whole course; the other nine are support. Don't spoil it by explaining it in advance — the comparison has to land on its own. Draw your own two dogs and notice that your symbol dog is from about 1987.
