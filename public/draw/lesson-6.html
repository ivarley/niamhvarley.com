Lesson 6

Everything is a box, a ball, a tube, or a cone

Skill
Construction
You'll need
A look around the room
Time
15 minutes

Warm-up

Ten ovals — they're about to become very useful.

2:00

The exercise

  1. Draw the four solids in 3D, big: a box, a ball, a tube (cylinder), a cone. The trick for the tube and the cone is that the circular ends are drawn as ovals, never as straight lines or as perfect circles.
  2. Now find eight real objects in the room and draw each one as nothing but those four solids stuck together. A lamp, a bottle, a dog, a chest of drawers, a person.
  3. No detail. No faces, no patterns, no texture. Just the solids.

What to look for

Almost nothing in the world isn't made of these four things.

Done means

Four solids plus eight objects built from them.

In Endless Paper

Once you've drawn a good tube, lasso it and duplicate it. Real artists reuse shapes constantly.

For the grown-up

This is the biggest single jump in making drawings look solid rather than flat, and it's the skill Mark Kistler's book drills hardest. It will feel like a step backwards to her because the drawings look cruder. It isn't.
